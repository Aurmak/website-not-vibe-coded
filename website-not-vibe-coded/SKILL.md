# Website, not vibe-coded

Load `RULES.md` in this folder and follow it in full. It is the owner's own instructions, learned across a real build, and it wins over default design and copy habits.

## New site or existing site?

- **New site:** follow section 1 of `RULES.md` from the start.
- **Existing site:** skip to section 1b. The site is the reference; inherit its brand, structure and claims. Run the audits first, report in three lists (Fixing, Recommend, Flagged), fix form without asking, and leave substance alone unless the owner asks.

## How to apply it

1. **Before anything:** read `RULES.md` section 1 (how to work). Plan first; build only when told; one page first; wait for the references and brand assets; never reverse an owner's choice without asking about that item on its own; a question is not an instruction; commit locally, push only when asked.
2. **Before writing copy:** section 2 (truth rules) and section 3 (copy voice). Run the banned-word and pattern scan on every draft, then read it aloud.
3. **Before any visual design:** section 4 (visual design) and the reference-handling rules. One accent colour, asymmetric left-aligned grid, no eyebrows, pills, blobs, nested cards or centred columns, no default fonts or component-library looks. Photo descriptions go in alt text, never in captions.
4. **While building pages:** section 5 (components) and section 6 (technical hygiene).
5. **After building:** section 7, the four audits in order (QA, design, SEO, copy), then the alignment check once a product exists.
6. **Before handover:** section 8, the final test: could someone who builds websites tell this was made with AI? Find the tell and remove it.

## Quick scan (run on every page of copy)

Reject on any of: an em dash or en dash used as punctuation; an exclamation mark; emoji; any word from the hype list in `RULES.md` section 3; "not just X, it's Y"; "whether you're"; "in today's"; "say goodbye to"; "imagine a"; a three-fragment stack; a colon-led headline; a rhetorical question opening a section; a statistic, quote or logo with no real source; a claim the product can't yet meet.

## Quick scan (run on every page of design)

Reject on any of: an eyebrow label; a decorative pill or badge; a gradient blob, glow or glass effect not in the references; a card inside a card; a page that is one centred column; an icon in a coloured box per feature; a caption under a photo; a second accent colour; a default font pairing; a hero without a large image or live element.
